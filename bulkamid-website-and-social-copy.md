## Bulkamid: A One-Time Office Procedure for Stress Incontinence

Leaking when you laugh, cough, sneeze, or exercise is called stress urinary incontinence (SUI). It happens when the muscles and tissues that support the bladder and urethra (the tube that carries urine out of the body) become weakened — commonly after pregnancy and childbirth, menopause, or aging. SUI affects up to one in three women. It is common, but it is not something you have to simply live with.

At The Urology Place in San Antonio, Dr. Naveen Kella now offers **Bulkamid**, a quick in-office procedure that treats stress leakage directly — no surgery, no mesh, and no hospital stay.

## Care from a Team You Can Talk To

Bladder leakage is personal, and many women put off getting help because the conversation feels uncomfortable. We understand. Your care is led by **Dr. Naveen Kella**, and **Savannah Eggers, PA**, works with you through the entire process — your evaluation, your procedure, and your follow-up. Many of our patients tell us that having a female provider on the team makes it easier to talk openly about symptoms and goals. Our job is to listen first, then match the treatment to you.

## Step One: A Proper Evaluation

Not all bladder leakage is the same. Some people leak with activity (stress incontinence), some leak with a sudden strong urge to urinate (urge incontinence), and many have both (mixed incontinence). The right treatment depends on which type you have.

Your visit may include:

- A conversation about your symptoms and how they affect your daily life
- A physical exam
- A urine test to rule out infection
- A check of how well your bladder empties
- Sometimes, a bladder diary or additional bladder testing

We will then review all of your options with you — from exercises and lifestyle changes to office procedures and surgery — so you can choose what fits your goals.

## What Is Bulkamid?

Bulkamid is a soft, water-based gel (about 97.5% water) that is injected into the wall of the urethra. The gel adds "bulk" so the urethra can close more tightly, helping prevent leaks when you cough, sneeze, or exercise. Bulkamid was approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) in 2020. **It is not a mesh**, and it does not involve any incisions.

**Who might it help?**
- Women with stress incontinence, or mixed incontinence where stress leakage is the main problem
- Women who want to avoid or delay surgery, or prefer a shorter recovery
- Women who still have leakage after a previous incontinence surgery
- Women who may still want to have children in the future

## What Is the Procedure Like?

- It is done **in our office** and usually takes about 10–15 minutes
- A numbing gel or local anesthetic is used to keep you comfortable
- A thin camera is placed into the urethra, and the gel is injected in a few small spots
- Most people go home shortly afterward and return to normal daily activities within a day or two

## What Results Can I Expect?

In clinical studies, about two out of three women or more reported being cured or improved at one year, and studies following women for up to 7 years have shown lasting benefit for many. Some women need a second injection (a "top-up") to get the best result — this is normal, and we will discuss it with you.

## What Are the Risks?

Serious complications are uncommon. Possible side effects include:
- Temporary discomfort or burning with urination
- A small amount of blood in the urine for a short time
- Urinary tract infection (a few percent of patients)
- Temporary difficulty emptying the bladder (a few percent of patients); this usually resolves quickly

## Other Options We Offer

We believe in matching treatment to the person. Other options for SUI include:
- **Pelvic floor physical therapy** and Kegel exercises — a proven first step
- **Lifestyle changes**, such as weight loss, managing fluid and caffeine intake, and treating chronic cough
- **Vaginal support devices** (pessaries)
- **Surgical procedures**, such as a sling, for people who want the most durable fix or have not improved with other treatments

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does insurance cover Bulkamid?**
Bulkamid is commonly covered by insurance when medically appropriate. Our staff can review your coverage and costs with you before treatment.

**Does the procedure hurt?**
A numbing gel or local anesthetic is used, and most patients find the 10–15 minute procedure very tolerable.

**Can I combine treatments?**
Yes. Some patients use pelvic floor therapy or Emsella along with Bulkamid. We can help you build a plan.

**Will I be able to have surgery later if needed?**
Yes. Bulkamid does not prevent you from having surgery later.
